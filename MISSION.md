# Flowstate — Mission

_Revised August 2026. Supersedes the "whole-life planning app" framing in README.md._

## The one-liner

**Flowstate is the only tool that helps you decide what to stop doing.**

Every other tool helps you manage work you've already accepted. Flowstate helps you not accept
the wrong work — and then get the work you did accept actually done.

## What it is

An operating system for a solo founder or a very small service business. It runs the business —
projects, time, invoicing, client onboarding — and sits a priority layer on top of it, so the
hours you spend go where you said they should.

It is, functionally, **your first hire.** The standard advice for a solo founder is that the
first person you hire is a bookkeeper, then an admin — because those tasks are repeatable,
low-judgment, and not client-facing, so delegating them saves the whole hour with near-zero
client risk. That's exactly the work Flowstate absorbs, at the stage where you can't yet justify
hiring either one.

## Who it's for

B2B service founders and freelancers who bill for their time and want to grow the business and
their own skills at the same time.

**Built for 1. Works up to ~5. Breaks at 10, on purpose.**

Above that you need real role-based permissions, approval chains, cross-person capacity planning,
PTO, and an HR-shaped thing — a different product, competing with companies that have fifty
engineers. The ceiling is a positioning asset, not a limitation.

## The problem

1. **Everything lives in a different tool.** Time in one place, invoices in another, tasks in a
   third, goals in a doc nobody opens. The numbers never reconcile, so none of them are trusted.
2. **Backlogs only grow.** No tool ever asks whether you still care about something. A list that
   only accumulates becomes a guilt machine, and eventually you stop opening it.
3. **Priorities live on a wall, not in the day.** You set a quarterly goal, and by week three
   nothing about Tuesday morning is different because of it.
4. **The wrong yes costs a quarter.** The decision that most determines how a quarter goes is
   which work you agree to — and it's made in an hour of excitement with no system behind it.

## What it does not do

Flowstate is not a wellness app, a habit tracker, a journal, or a life-planning system. It does
not manage your relationships, your fitness, or your mood. Those were cut deliberately.

The one number that survives from that world is **hours worked per week** — kept because in a
company of one you are the supply, and sustained overwork is delivery risk on every active
client project. It sits next to effective hourly rate, where it reads as a pricing signal and a
hire trigger rather than as self-care.

## The model

### Direction and Targets

**Directions** are qualitative, durable, and survive across quarters.
_"We serve early-stage teams shipping production software. We don't take design-only work."_
A direction is never measured. It is **applied** — its whole job is to let you say no fast.

**Targets** are measurable and time-boxed to a horizon.
_"Sign 2 new clients by Dec 31." "$X booked in Q4." "Flowstate v1 shipped."_
Targets tell you whether you're winning. Directions tell you what game you're playing.

Two rules, both non-negotiable:

1. **Every target belongs to a direction.** A number with no direction behind it is how you end
   up optimizing something that doesn't matter.
2. **Cap them.** One or two directions. Three targets per quarter, maximum. Twelve priorities is
   zero priorities. The cap _is_ the closing of doors.

### The hierarchy

**Direction → Target → Project → Task.**

Tasks inherit their goal from their project. A solo founder has 8–15 live projects, not 400, so
the linkage is set a handful of times a quarter — and even then the tool proposes it at project
creation (_"This looks like it serves 'Increase revenue' — yes / no / different goal"_) rather
than asking you to tag anything by hand.

A project may instead be tagged **Maintenance**, which requires no goal. That's a legitimate
answer, not a failure.

### Horizons

The sorting criterion is not the size of a thing. It is **how fast you'd want to know you're off
track** — review something faster than its feedback loop and it becomes noise; slower, and you
find out too late to steer.

| Horizon     | What it holds                                                                                                        | Review    |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------- |
| **Quarter** | The bet. Directions, strategy, learning roadmap, tool spend, compliance.                                             | Quarterly |
| **Month**   | The money and the delivery. Revenue, effective rate, invoices sent and unpaid, project health, the hire trigger.     | Monthly   |
| **Week**    | The steering. Pipeline, outreach, features shipped, the Sweep, and _"am I still working on the right thing at all."_ | Weekly    |
| **Day**     | The doing.                                                                                                           | —         |

### Reviewed vs. triggered

Not everything belongs in a horizon. **Reviewed** items live in a review cadence. **Triggered**
items live on a tickler and stay invisible until their moment — taxes, LLC filings, insurance
renewals, contract expirations. You don't want to review compliance quarterly; you want to be
pinged and otherwise never think about it.

### The personal category

Personal work is a **category, not a subsystem.** One project, tagged Maintenance, no goal
required, recurring tasks allowed. It appears on your day and in your time budget — because
Tuesday afternoon does not distinguish between drafting a proposal and taking the car in, and a
tool that can't see both will confidently promise you capacity you don't have. It is excluded
from the entire goal layer: no targets, no progress, no reviews, no nagging.

**The rule: personal work may be held, never managed.** The day a personal goals view gets built,
the line has been crossed.

## The mechanics

**The Budget** — denominated in **time, never task count**. You declare the tilt (_Q4 is 70%
business_) and the day shows a live bar. Task count punishes quick wins and rewards busywork;
time does the opposite. Six ten-minute errands barely move it. A three-hour detour shows up
instantly. It never blocks you — it makes the trade visible at the moment you're making it.

**The Toll** — charged **per project, not per task**. Creating a project costs you one sentence:
_what is this in service of?_ If the honest answer is "nothing," it goes to the Abyss instead of
onto today. Paid a handful of times a quarter, not forty times a week.

**The Ledger** — every two weeks. _"You said 70% business. You spent 41%."_ No nagging during the
day, no interruption. Its power is that it's unarguable: you tracked the time yourself. Biweekly
because monthly is long enough to lose a month to the wrong thing.

**The Sweep** — weekly. The tool hands you what's gone stale and makes you rule on each item:
**drop, park, or keep.** This is closing doors, made routine, and it's the mechanic the mission
is named after. The same three buckets apply at every altitude — tasks, projects, features, and
the goals themselves.

**The Filter** — the highest-leverage feature in the product. When a lead comes in, an intake
link goes out (eight questions, two minutes of their time). The answers get scored against your
active Direction and Target, and you get **pursue / negotiate / decline**, with reasons, before
you've spent an hour on a call. Being in or out fast is a system.

The filter asks what you ask when you're thinking clearly, plus what you skip when you're
excited or the pipeline looks thin:

- _Fit_ — How long will it take? Will they pay my rate? Is it in my target industry? How similar
  is it to work I've done? Do I want to work with these people?
- _Risk_ — Have they bought this kind of work before? What happened with their last contractor?
  Am I talking to the person who signs? Did they name a budget or dodge it? Will they pay a
  deposit? Can I write what "done" looks like in one sentence?
- _Strategy_ — Does this produce a case study or referral in my target industry, and would I be
  allowed to talk about it? Does it build a capability on my learning roadmap, or a dead-end
  skill? **What do I have to say no to in order to say yes?**

Overrides are always allowed and always logged with a reason.

**The Tickler** — business compliance only. Taxes, filings, insurance, contract renewals.

**The Learning Roadmap** — a quarter-horizon goal in its own right. Your effective rate rises
when your skills do, so learning is treated as a business investment, not a hobby.

## Product laws

1. **Flowstate drafts. You sign.** The tool prepares invoices, proposals, folders, and
   recommendations. It never sends anything to a client, changes a price, or accepts work on
   your behalf. The never-delegate list — strategy, client relationships, pricing, final review —
   is also the never-automate list. Every AI action is reversible.
2. **Menus, not assignments.** Past-you setting the priority and present-you choosing today's
   tasks in light of it feels like autonomy. Past-you assigning today's tasks feels like a boss.
   The constraint is identical; the framing is everything.
3. **Interruptions must be earned by evidence, never fired on a timer.** A reminder that is wrong
   more often than it's right teaches reflexive dismissal, and dismissal is a habit that forms
   faster than any habit it was meant to build. Knowing when _not_ to fire is the entire
   difference between day 3 and day 300.
4. **Time is the score.** Not tasks completed. Not streaks.
5. **A parked feature is a door left ajar.** Park only what is finished and harmless. Kill the
   rest.
6. **Build for one user.** Don't design for a market. But don't make irreversible decisions
   against a future one either — `user_id` and `org_id` exist from day one; nothing else about
   multi-tenancy does.

## Roles

|                                       | Owner | Partner | Member    |
| ------------------------------------- | ----- | ------- | --------- |
| Their own projects and tasks          | ✅    | ✅      | ✅        |
| All company projects                  | ✅    | ✅      | ❌        |
| Rates, revenue, invoices              | ✅    | ✅      | ❌        |
| Owner's hours                         | ✅    | ✅      | ❌        |
| Hours logged on a shared project      | ✅    | ✅      | ✅        |
| Company Directions and Targets        | ✅    | ✅      | ✅ (view) |
| Own learning roadmap and growth goals | ✅    | ✅      | ✅        |

Members' time and progress roll up to the Owner in a **separate team view** — never mixed into
the Owner's own reviews. Reviewing your own quarter and managing someone else's are different
jobs and shouldn't share a screen.

Member onboarding — the guided process for helping a new person define their own growth goals —
is **parked**. Good idea, not v1.

## v1 scope — the definition of DONE

Built outside billable hours. Ships when these work, and not one feature later.

1. Project tracking
2. Task tracking
3. Time tracking against projects and tasks
4. Time reporting and invoicing, with billing thresholds and proposed invoices for review
5. Directions and Targets across quarter / month / week horizons, with the Budget, the Ledger,
   and the Sweep
6. Client onboarding automation — one action creates the folders, starter contracts, the
   project, and its time-tracking setup
7. The Filter and its intake link
8. Business tickler for compliance
9. Personal category (Maintenance projects and recurring tasks) — no goal layer

**Later, explicitly not now:** team members and everything in the roles table beyond Owner,
member onboarding, the Top 3 gate, anything resembling a wellness feature, and any UI for
multi-tenancy.

**The build-vs-buy rule:** if a feature takes 20 hours to build and saves 15 minutes a month,
it doesn't get built. Do it by hand or buy it.

## The trap to watch

Building Flowstate feels exactly like business work. It's productive, it's hard, it's yours, and
it produces visible progress — which makes it the most seductive available way to spend a quarter
without launching anything. The scope in this document is the guardrail. Anything not in it is a
door that has been closed.
