What Tab and Shift+Tab actually do

In every model, tab and ⇧tab move a row in and out one level. They differ on whether a row can change kind — task into phase, or phase into task.

This matters because the two are not the same object. A task carries completion, logged time entries, tags, priority and recurrence. A phase carries a date range, an estimate, and children. There is no conversion operation on the server, so changing kind means creating one row and deleting the other, and whatever the old row carried has nowhere to land.

Option A · recommended

Move only, never convert

Indent reparents. Kind is fixed at creation.

  • A task outdented past the top level becomes a loose task on the project, which is a real state the schema already has.
  • A phase indented becomes a sub-phase of the row above it.
  • Nothing is ever created or destroyed by a keystroke, so nothing can be lost by one.
  • Making a task into a phase is still possible, as an explicit action on the row that says what it will do.
  • Departs from the deck, which said indenting a top-level row turns it into a task.

  • Matches the deck's promise most of the time, since a row you just typed is usually empty.

  • The interaction is uniform: indent always means what the deck said.
  • You are never surprised, because you are asked.
  • A confirm dialog on a keystroke is a contradiction. Tab is meant to be fast; a modal in its path makes restructuring feel dangerous.
  • Really does destroy time entries, which feed the burn signal, the Ledger and your invoices.
Where I land

Option A. The deck's rule read well on paper because it treated phases and tasks as one thing at different depths. In the schema they are two tables with different cargo, and a keystroke that quietly converts between them puts logged time — the thing your invoices are built from — one stray Shift+Tab away from gone.

A keeps what actually made the outline good: no syntax, structure by position, one keystroke to reorganise. It gives up only the ability to change a row's kind by accident, and offers an explicit action for the rare time you meant it.

Worth noticing that this is the schema telling you something. Phases and tasks feel like one hierarchy while you are planning, and they are not. If they ever should be, that is a data-model decision, not a keybinding.
