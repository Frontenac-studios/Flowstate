One thing your picks left open

What "62% done" is counting

5D and 6B both put a percent on screen, and today that percent is completed tasks divided by total tasks. MISSION rejects that denominator by name: the Budget is time-denominated precisely because "task count punishes quick wins and rewards busywork." You picked both anyway, which is a reasonable thing to want — you do want to know how much is left. So the question isn't whether to show it, it's what to divide by.

Option i

Count tasks

What's built today.

62% done18 of 29 tasks
what moves it
"Renew the domain" — 5 minutes — moves it 3.4%
"Rebuild the rollup query" — 6 hours — moves it 3.4%
  • Already computed, already shipped, nothing to build.
  • Always available, even with no estimates anywhere.
  • A five-minute task and a six-hour task move the bar identically.
  • Clearing small things feels like progress on the thing that's actually late.
  • Contradicts the denominator the Budget exists to establish.
Option ii

Weight by estimated hours

Same bar, honest denominator.

41% done10h of 24h estimated
what moves it
"Renew the domain" — 5 minutes — moves it 0.3%
"Rebuild the rollup query" — 6 hours — moves it 25%
  • Sum the estimates of finished tasks, divide by the total estimate. The bar now means the same thing the Budget means.
  • Sits directly above the budget bar on the same band, in the same unit — the two become comparable rather than two unrelated percentages.
  • Same 62%-vs-41% gap is itself information: it says the work you've cleared was the cheap half.
  • Needs estimates. With none, it falls back to task count and says so ("18 of 29 tasks, no estimates yet").
  • A real change to the aggregation, not just a label — roughly a day.
Why I'm raising it rather than deciding it

The gap in the mockups is the argument. The same project is 62% done by task count and 41% done by hours, and the second number is the one that tells you the truth about what Thursday looks like. On a status band whose whole purpose is "how much is left against how much budget is gone," two bars in two different units invite a comparison that doesn't hold.

The counterargument is real too: option ii is only as good as your estimates, and you don't currently estimate everything. Which is why the fallback matters — when a project has no estimates, it says so instead of pretending.

Build order

What to ship, in what order

Ordered so nothing is broken in between, and so the biggest build is de-risked before it's committed to.

  1. Create dialog (1A) and the palette entry. Two evenings. Leave the wizard reachable from ⋯ but stop it auto-opening. Nothing else changes yet.
  2. Projects index as a status view (6B). Layout over data that already exists. Ships the first visible win, and gives project state a display before it has an editor.
  3. Plan mode, read-only (2D). The outline as a view: board data flattened, dates and hours in columns. Confirm the two views agree before betting the planning flow on it.
  4. Plan mode, editable, plus the details strip (2D + 3B). Tab, Shift+Tab, Enter, inline dates and estimates. State gets its control here. Delete the wizard the day this lands.
  5. The status band (5B + 5D) on the project page, in whichever denominator you land on above.
  6. Composer fixes (4A). One submit key, printed destination phase, grammar in words, the ;tag contradiction resolved. Small, and can slot in anywhere after step 4.
  7. Today signals (7B + 7C). Belonging labels first — trivial. The health line after the burn thresholds are settled by step 5.
  8. Triage strip becomes a queue (8). Routes to Later or the Abyss, count reaches zero.
